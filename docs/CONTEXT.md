## 1. Product summary

Clinic Hub is the patient and clinic portal pair for The Schoeman Clinic (hormone, longevity and functional medicine, London). The MVP is built around the bloods loop: order, Stripe payment and barcode, results by lab feed or PDF import with PA review, doctor review and release, then the patient sees the trend. GoHighLevel keeps messaging, reminders, tasks, calendar, invoices and marketing. Semble keeps consultation notes and prescribing for now. [2026-11] Since the clinic's decisions of 11 November, clinical notes are written in Clinic Hub too (drafts, signed and locked, corrected by a new version; no Semble sync), staff assign education courses to patients, and patients type in wearable readings (Finding 74 on the Semble wording). Prescribing goes through SignatureRx per `CLAUDE.md` and is not built. Each staff member can connect their own Google Calendar and see and edit it at `/clinic/calendar`. [2026-11, `feat/clinic-build-2`] Since the clinic's decisions of 11 and 12 November: **appointments are booked in Clinic Hub** (patients with their care team's clinicians, staff for any patient they can open, inside each clinician's weekly hours; the booking goes into the clinician's Google Calendar if connected; patients cannot cancel, the team does); a **signed note is final** (no corrections); a doctor, nurse or health coach **records a call** from the patient's record and the AssemblyAI transcript arrives as a draft note (replacing Fathom); and patient messages (result ready, appointment booked or cancelled) go **by email and WhatsApp through GoHighLevel** when it is connected, otherwise by email through Resend. Bookings are not synced to or from GoHighLevel or Semble (Finding 89). [2026-11, `feat/finalise-points`] Patients (and guardians who can act) **ask the clinic** questions in the portal and the conversation continues in replies on both sides; the team **upload and share letters, referrals and other documents**, which patients open in the portal; patients record **consent choices** (care, partner laboratories, research); and the clinic's patient record shows allergies, diagnoses, medicines (read only), documents, open questions and, for doctors, safeguarding alerts, with a doctors-only safeguarding list. Questions replace the draft's plan of replying in GoHighLevel (Finding 112).

### 4.1 Principles

- Row-level security is **enabled and forced** on every table in `public` (loop at the end of the integrations migration). No `FOR ALL` policies. Each table has **one permissive policy per command**, combining roles with OR, so the `multiple_permissive_policies` advisor stays quiet [2026-11].
- All new objects start closed: default privileges revoke everything from `public`, `anon` and `authenticated`. The grants migration then revokes and re-grants explicitly. `anon` holds nothing (there are no public pages). Column-level grants are used for insert and update.
- No `ON DELETE CASCADE` or `SET NULL` anywhere (every foreign key is `on delete restrict`, checked by a test). `authenticated` can delete only from `staff_locations` and `lab_panel_markers`. The service role has **no delete or truncate** on any clinical table, including `profiles`, `user_roles`, `webhook_events` and `integration_sync_log` [2026-11]. Clinical records are archived with `archived_at`; accounts are suspended through `profiles.status`.
- `webhook_events`, `integration_sync_log` and `audit_log` have no policies and no grants to `authenticated`: no table access. Since `20261110120100` [2026-11] two functions reach `audit_log` for signed-in people: `log_record_view` writes a `viewed` row (anyone with the patient in scope since `20261112120100` [2026-11]) and `activity_log` lets an admin read it (4.7c).
- Policies call helpers as `(select private.has_role('x'))` and `(select auth.uid())` so Postgres evaluates them once per statement. Policies that take a row value (`private.in_scope(patient_id)`, `can_act_for`, `document_gate_open`) are called directly.
- Every function sets `search_path`. No `SECURITY DEFINER` function exists in `public`; a test enforces it.
- Writes that must not come from a browser are made by server code with the service role (`lib/supabase/admin.ts`), after the server has checked who is asking. Several tables therefore have a select policy only.

### 4.2 Roles and access levels

`app_role` is `patient, doctor, pa, health_coach, nurse, admin` (`nurse` added in `20261110120000` [2026-11]). There is **no guardian role** [2026-11]: a guardian holds no role and is defined by a verified `patient_guardians` row (`verified_by` not null) with `can_view` (and `can_act` to pay, request or ask). A person can hold more than one role (the seed has a doctor who is also a patient).

| Group | Roles | Helper |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical staff | doctor, **nurse** (since `20261112120100` [2026-11]), PA, health coach | `private.is_clinical_staff()` |
| Team | doctor, PA, admin | `private.is_team()` |
| Admin | admin | `private.has_role('admin')` |
| Who may open a patient's record | PAs and admins: every patient. Doctors, nurses and health coaches: patients whose care team they are currently on | `private.in_scope(patient_id)` |

**Access by care team** [2026-11, `20261112120100_access_by_care_team.sql`, the clinic's answers to knowledge base questions 1 to 3 and 7]. `private.in_scope()` is no longer "any clinical staff": it is true for an admin or a PA for every patient, and for a doctor, nurse or health coach only while a `care_team` row links their staff profile to the patient with `since <= current_date` and `until` empty or after today (so setting `until` to today removes access at once). Because every patient-record policy already called `in_scope`, this one change narrows them all. Inside their scope, staff read the **whole record**, including unreleased reports, results and documents (the release gate now admits anyone in scope, 4.5). Still limited by role on top: safeguarding alerts (doctors in scope), diagnoses and medications (written by doctors), release of results (doctors), care-team edits (PA, admin, doctor), clinical notes (written by doctors, nurses and health coaches).

**Nurse** [2026-11]: since `20261112120100` the nurse is clinical staff and works like a doctor on the patients of their care team, except safeguarding, diagnoses, prescribing and release: the nurse reads the record, enters results and sends files for entry (`enter_report`, `register_import`), works the import queue, adds and changes allergies, adds and changes `documents` rows, and writes clinical notes. A nurse can be put on a care team (`care_relationship` value `nurse`, `20261112120000`). The `documents` **storage** insert policy was not widened, so a nurse cannot upload the file itself (Finding 71).

**Admin** [2026-11]: **an admin now reads every patient's clinical record** (`in_scope` is true for admins), including unreleased results, documents and signed notes, as the clinic decided (knowledge base questions 2 and 27). An admin is still not clinical staff: no role-specific write (diagnoses, medications, release, notes) opens to them, but they edit care teams, assign education courses and change question status (`is_team()` and `in_scope`). The earlier rule "admin reads no patient record" no longer holds; neither does "the health coach reads released results and documents only": a health coach on a patient's care team reads everything an in-scope reader reads except safeguarding alerts, and every care-team member reads that patient's care team (`is_team() or in_scope`). The health coach still reads no staff profiles, import queue, invitations or staff locations.

**Own record [2026-11, `a55db7b`, edit to `20261112120100`]:** `in_scope` is false for the caller's own patient record and for any patient in their `my_patient_ids()` (people they look after), so a team member who is also a patient sees their own record only as a patient does (S-112). A trigger, `care_team_reject_self`, refuses putting a staff member on their own care team. The care-team read policy is now `in_scope(patient_id)` alone (the `is_team()` half was dropped).

A staff role counts only while the person has an active, unarchived `staff_profiles` row and the account is not suspended, so offboarding takes effect immediately without editing roles. A patient role also needs a non-suspended account.

Access levels by table: own account (profiles, notification tables), patient record (clinical staff in scope plus the patient and verified guardians), team only, doctors only (safeguarding alerts; diagnoses and medications are doctor-written), reference (readable by anyone signed in, admin-written), server only.

### 4.5 The release gate

Patients and guardians see a result, a report or a lab PDF **only once the report is released**; staff with the patient in scope see everything, released or not. [2026-11] Since `20261112120100` the `results`, `lab_reports` and `documents` select policies are "own and released, or `in_scope`", so a health coach on the care team and every admin also read unreleased data (before, the health coach was held to released data and admins saw none). The gate rests on `lab_reports.released_at` and on composite foreign keys that make a result and a source PDF belong to the same patient as their report. `private.report_is_released(report_id)` guards `results`; `private.document_gate_open(document_id, kind)` guards `documents`: a patient's own upload is always visible, and any file used by an unreleased report stays hidden even if filed under another kind. A document is also visible to a patient only when `shared_with_patient` is true and it is not archived.

It is in `private`, outside the Data API, and has no RLS: the revokes are its protection.
- `private.auth_throttle_hit(_keys text[], _limits integer[], _window_seconds integer) returns boolean`: `SECURITY DEFINER`, `search_path = ''`. Purges expired rows; returns false if any bucket is full, counting nothing, so a refused attempt cannot use up another bucket; otherwise adds one to every bucket and returns true. Concurrent calls can overshoot a limit slightly.
- `public.auth_throttle_hit(...)`: `SECURITY INVOKER` wrapper, execute granted to `service_role` only. Called from `lib/auth/throttle.ts`.

**Session limits** (`lib/supabase/session-cookies.ts`, enforced in `lib/supabase/proxy.ts`) [2026-11, uncommitted]:

| Kind | Total | Idle |
|---|---|---|
| Staff | 12 hours | 30 minutes |
| Patient (and guardian) | 7 days | 2 hours |

- `ch_session_kind` holds the kind and `ch_last_seen` the last request time; both are **signed** [2026-11, uncommitted]. The value is `value.signature`, where the signature is HMAC-SHA256 (Web Crypto, key `SESSION_COOKIE_SECRET`) over `cookie name|user id|value`, so a cookie cannot be edited, moved to another cookie or used by another account. A missing, unsigned, edited or foreign cookie reads as missing: a missing last-seen counts as expired and a missing kind gets the staff limits. If `SESSION_COOKIE_SECRET` is missing or under 32 characters, `getSessionEnv` throws and the proxy fails closed. Server code sets them through `startSessionCookies` in `lib/auth/session-start.ts`.
- `signIn` and `updatePassword` set it; staff limits win for someone who is also a patient, and a missing or unknown value gets the staff limits. Paths under `/clinic` always use the staff limits, whatever the cookie says.
- `ch_last_seen` is written at most once a minute. Sign-in and password change set it. A missing value, or one more than a minute in the future, counts as expired, so the visitor is signed out.
- The total runs from the latest sign-in time in the token's `amr` claim, which a refresh does not change, with `iat` as the fallback.
- A session past a limit is signed out locally, its `sb-*` and `ch_*` cookies are removed, and the visitor goes to `/login?error=expired`.
- `/auth/confirm` is exempt in the proxy, which leaves both `ch_` cookies alone there. The route itself calls `startSessionCookies(user id, null)`, which signs `ch_last_seen` as now and deletes `ch_session_kind`, only after `verifyOtp` or `exchangeCodeForSession` succeeds, because a verified link starts a new session (staff limits apply until the kind is known). A bad link changes nothing.
- Auth cookies and both `ch_` cookies are `HttpOnly`, `Secure` in production, `SameSite=Lax`, with a 7-day `maxAge`. `@supabase/ssr` overwrites `maxAge` with its 400-day default, so `withSessionMaxAge` sets it again on each write.

**Session guard** (`components/shell/session-guard.tsx`) [2026-11, uncommitted]. A Client Component rendered by `AppShell`, which has a new required prop `sessionKind` (`staff` or `patient`): the clinic layout passes `staff`, the portal layout passes `staff` for anyone with a staff role and `patient` otherwise. While the person is active (pointer, key, scroll, touch) it calls `router.refresh()` at most every 5 minutes, which keeps the idle clock moving; once the person stops for the session's idle limit, an idle timer calls `router.refresh()` so the proxy ends the session and sends them to `/login?error=expired`. Without it an abandoned screen stays readable. It renders nothing and makes no Supabase call.

**Content-Security-Policy** (`lib/csp.ts`, set in `lib/supabase/proxy.ts`) [2026-11, uncommitted]. The proxy makes a nonce for each request (`createNonce`), builds the policy with `contentSecurityPolicy(nonce)`, and sets it on every response (including redirects) and, with `x-nonce`, on the forwarded request headers so Next.js adds the nonce to its scripts. `script-src` is `'self' 'nonce-...' 'strict-dynamic'`, with `'unsafe-eval'` in development only. `style-src` still allows `'unsafe-inline'`, because React and Radix write style attributes a nonce cannot cover (`docs/SECURITY.md` S-12). Other directives: `default-src 'self'`, images from self, `data:`, `blob:` and the Supabase origin, `connect-src` self plus Supabase (and local websockets in development), `object-src 'none'`, `base-uri 'self'`, `form-action 'self'`, `frame-ancestors 'none'`. A nonce needs a fresh render, so `app/layout.tsx` awaits `connection()` and no page is statically rendered.
