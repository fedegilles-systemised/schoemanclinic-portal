## Executive summary

No CRITICAL finding, and no HIGH finding is open. A patient or guardian could not reach another patient's data or an unreleased result through PostgREST, RPC, embedded joins or Storage in any attack tried. `anon` reads nothing. Nobody can write their own role.

- **All five HIGH findings are resolved and held under attack:** the lab PDF gate (S-01), forged guardian links (S-02), client-made invitations (S-03), offboarding (S-04) and server updates of `staff_profiles` (S-05). Each fix leaves a smaller follow-on, recorded as its own LOW finding: S-38 and S-42 (S-43 is now resolved, see the next point).
- **The CSP now blocks injected script (S-12, resolved in the working tree).** Against a production build, every route answered with a CSP carrying a fresh nonce and `strict-dynamic`, with no `unsafe-eval`, and every `<script>` tag in the pages tested carried that request's nonce. A client-sent nonce or CSP header changed nothing. `style-src` still allows `'unsafe-inline'`, which cannot stop injected styling (details in S-12). One gap: paths the proxy matcher skips (`/foo.png`, `/favicon.ico` and similar) return the full HTML 404 page with no CSP at all (S-57).
- **Session limits can no longer be edited from the browser (S-31, still OPEN).** `ch_last_seen` and `ch_session_kind` are signed with `SESSION_COOKIE_SECRET`, bound to the cookie name and the account. Unsigned, truncated, malformed, re-valued, other-account and other-cookie values were all refused, and a staff session with a patient kind cookie is refused on `/portal`. The secret is required and the app fails closed without it (500 on every signed-in request). What remains is the copied-refresh-token route, which needs Supabase's Pro-plan limits, and replay of signed values inside their window (also across sessions of one account).
- **The idle timer works end to end (S-48, resolved).** With its 30-minute delay shortened to 3 seconds, an expired staff session was sent to `/login?error=expired` with the cookies cleared and the screen replaced. The real 30-minute wait and a sleeping device were not tested (S-61).
- **S-10 is closed technically, and the decision stays open.** Every write policy on a patient's record now asks `private.in_scope()`, and with scope narrowed in a rolled-back transaction each altered policy held. `in_scope` is still true for all clinical staff until the clinic answers (PRD open question 1), so everyone still reads every patient. Two reads still ignore scope (S-58).
- **Invitations hold against every direct attack tried (tenth run), and S-43 is resolved.** `anon`, a patient, a doctor, a health coach, a PA and an admin were all refused on the five server functions through the Data API (401 or 403), and `get_invitable_patients` answered only for the PA and the admin (empty for the rest). `token_hash` cannot be read, the table cannot be written by any signed-in role, and `private` is not exposed. Forged form fields (`_inviter`, `invited_by`, `role`, `token_hash`), an admin-only kind from a PA, a team role `patient`, a patient the record of whom already has an account, and cross-origin posts all failed. Acceptance is one locked transaction: two simultaneous accepts of one link gave one success and one "already used"; expired, cancelled, used, suspended-invitee, deactivated-inviter, inviter-without-the-role and wrong-address cases were all refused. Links are 43-character random tokens, stored only as a SHA-256, opened by a `GET` that sets a cookie and changes nothing, so a mail scanner cannot use one up (S-36 for this link). **What is still open is who may be invited and what the link proves:** the link is the only check on a patient's or guardian's record (S-74), one PA or admin can bind any record to any address (S-75, S-42), and an acceptance that fails late for another reason could still leave an orphan account (S-77, reduced). **Update [2026-11]:** `20261105130000_invitation_identity.sql` adds the patient's date of birth at acceptance (five wrong dates cancel the link), a confirmation when the address differs from the record, and notices for doctor, admin and can-act guardian invitations. S-74 is reduced (a team link is still the only check), S-75 is eased, and S-86 and S-87 record what the new controls do not cover.

## Documented decisions

- [2026-11] **Admin is deliberately not clinical staff.** `is_clinical_staff` excludes admin, and tests confirm no clinical reads. Admins do read all `profiles`, `care_team` links and `invitations.patient_id` (verified: 11, 12 and 3 rows), so they can see who is a patient and who cares for them. See S-03 for the route around this decision. Open question 2 in `docs/PRD.md`.
- [2026-11] **`in_scope` is open until the clinic answers.** See S-10. Open question 1 in `docs/PRD.md`.
- [2026-11] **Roles live in `user_roles`, written only by server code,** never read from the JWT.
- [2026-11] **Definer code lives in `private`,** not exposed through the Data API. `authenticated` needs `usage` on it because policies call the helpers. Hidden columns are served through invoker wrappers in `public`.
- [2026-11] **No hard deletes.** No `delete` for `authenticated` on clinical tables, and none for `service_role` either. No delete policy on Storage.
- [2026-11] **A staff role needs an active staff profile.** `private.is_active_staff` is checked by `has_role`, `is_clinical_staff` and `is_team`, so deactivating someone ends their access without touching their roles.
- [2026-11] **No browser client.** All Supabase calls run on the server and auth cookies are `HttpOnly`. Any later browser feature, such as direct uploads or Realtime, needs its own design. The Supabase origins in `connect-src` can probably go.
- [2026-11] **Session limits are enforced by the app.** 12 hours and 30 minutes idle for staff, 7 days and 2 hours for patients, read from a signed `amr` claim and from `ch_*` cookies signed with `SESSION_COOKIE_SECRET`. The cookies were unsigned until the fifth run. The residual risk (a copied refresh token, which only Supabase's Pro-plan limits end) is recorded in S-31, and no human has yet formally accepted it, so S-31 stays OPEN.
- [2026-11] **CAPTCHA (S-32) and MFA for staff (S-11) are deferred by the owner** until the clinic has the accounts. Both stay OPEN, and both must be done before real patient data is held.
- [2026-11] **Service-role writes must name an actor or a system job.** Server code sets `app.actor_id` (a profile id) or `app.system_actor` (a job name) with `set_config(..., true)` inside the one transaction that writes. A write without either is refused for the `service_role` role only. See S-07 and S-62.
- [2026-11] **One hosted environment for now,** with the local stack as development. Owner's decision. See the checklist and S-56.
- [2026-11] **The CSP is set per request by the proxy,** with a nonce and `strict-dynamic`, no `unsafe-eval` in production and `style-src 'unsafe-inline'` (React and Radix write style attributes). Every page is rendered per request (`await connection()` in the root layout) so each can carry its nonce. See S-12 and S-57.

- [2026-11] **Invitations: who invites whom.** Admins send every kind; PAs send patient and guardian invitations; doctors, health coaches and patients send none. Links work once and for 3 days (`private.invitation_lifetime()`; the table allows 14). There is no second approver for any kind (PRD open question). Sign-up is invitation only; an existing account accepts by signing in, and its email must equal the invited address. Accounts made from a link are created confirmed, on the strength of the link reaching the address. Acceptance is audited under the inviter. S-74 and S-75 say what this leaves open. [2026-11] Patient and guardian links also need the patient's date of birth, with five tries per link (the fifth cancels it); a patient invitation to an address other than the record's needs the sender to confirm; doctor and admin invitations notify the other admins, and a guardian who can act notifies the other PAs. Team links need no second fact.
- [2026-11] **The service role is used for invitations, and nowhere else for screens.** `sendInvitation`, `resendInvitation`, `cancelInvitation`, the accept page's lookup and the three accept actions call the service-role client after checking who is asking in code; each database function checks the named person again (`may_invite`, `person_has_role`) and sets `app.actor_id`. The list on the Team screen and the patient picker are read through the person's own session.
- [2026-11] **Profile: who can do what.** Everyone changes their own name, phone and photo, and requests an email change (with the current password) and a password change (with the current password, which signs other devices out). Only doctors store a signature, in its own private bucket with one fixed name. Nobody deletes a photo or signature file. Patient contact details on the record are read-only in the app. These are the app's rules; the Data API is the same people with fewer questions (S-37, S-68, S-69, S-70, S-71).
- [2026-11] **Photos are readable by all signed-in people for active staff, and by the team for patients.** The `avatars` policy is intended for names and photos of staff in the staff directory. Patient photos going to every doctor, PA and admin regardless of scope is the current behaviour (S-70); whether that is right is a PRD question.
- [2026-11] **Results: entry by PA or doctor, release by a doctor, writes only through functions.** The tables still give signed-in people no insert and no update of reports or results (apart from `doctor_comment`, doctors only). Entry (`enter_report`), registering a file for entry (`register_import`) and release (`release_report`) are `SECURITY DEFINER` functions in `private` with `SECURITY INVOKER` wrappers in `public`, so the checks run inside the function. A patient or guardian may register their own uploaded file for entry. The database converts units and sets the flag; nobody edits them afterwards. The clinic's unit list and ranges are still to come (S-93). Whether released comments can change and whether a doctor may release their own results are open (S-89, S-92).
- [2026-11] **Result-ready email: the service role reads one address, after release.** `tellPatientByEmail` runs only when `release_report` has succeeded, and the service role is used only to read `profiles.email` and `status` for the patient's own user id (the screen reads use the doctor's session). The email has the first name and a link to `/portal/results`; results are never emailed. It also reads the patient's `notification_preferences` row (topic `results`, channel `email`) and sends nothing if `enabled` is false; no row counts as on. A failure does not undo the release; the doctor is told. See S-96 (resolved), S-97 and S-98.

- [2026-11] **A nurse has no patient access until the clinic decides.** `private.is_clinical_staff` does not list `nurse`; a nurse can be invited as a team member and signs in to `/clinic`, but every patient table returns nothing. Owner's decision, recorded as given; the clinic's answer needs new policy work (S-105).
- [2026-11] **Who opened a record is written to the audit log, and an admin reads it.** `log_record_view` writes `viewed` rows with the actor, role, entity, record id and patient id, and never a value; `activity_log` shows an admin who, what and which patient (name and clinic reference). Owner's decision. The report page fails closed if the row cannot be written (S-102), the log names only record kinds on a fixed list (S-100) and only real records of that patient (S-101); coverage is still the report page only (S-103).
- [2026-11] **Staff Google Calendar: one encrypted token per person, read only by the server.** As built (uncommitted): the refresh token is encrypted in the app with `GOOGLE_TOKEN_ENCRYPTION_KEY` before it is stored, signed-in people see only whether they are connected and to which address, and the service role reads the token and writes only through two service-role-only functions that log the change under that person. Disconnecting deletes the row rather than archiving it, because it is a credential, not a clinical record; this is the one deliberate exception to "no hard deletes", and it is in the audit log. Event changes are not logged (PRD). Which Google accounts may be connected is not decided (S-106).
